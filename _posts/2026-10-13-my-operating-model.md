These reflect my actionable operating principles: questions I use to change decisions and behavior on a day to day basis at Wispr.

**1. What's the desired outcome and the fastest path to it?**
{: .operating-model-label }

- What outcome are we trying to get to? What would count as success?
- What can we cut, simplify or reuse to get there faster? Don't reinvent what already works.
- Optimize for time to the ultimate outcome. Often that means shipping incrementally, learning quickly, then making what works dependable.

**2. Who's the DRI?**
{: .operating-model-label }

- Can everyone involved name the same directly responsible individual (DRI)? The DRI owns the outcome and makes the call.
- The DRI notices when work stalls or the experience breaks, drives the fix and stays responsible after shipping. Nothing should dangle.
- Keep teams small and decision makers few. Hold one another accountable and flag commitments at risk before they slip.

**3. Diagnose the problem**
{: .operating-model-label }

- Can you explain the problem without naming your proposed solution?
- If we're moving too slowly, why? Check whether the work needs simplifying, whether we're solving the right problem and what's actually blocking progress.

**4. Assume good intent and approach conversations with curiosity**
{: .operating-model-label }

- If someone says something that makes no sense to you, find out what they see that you don't. Understand the disagreement, then disagree and commit.
- Use VIEW: vulnerability about your uncertainty; impartiality about whose idea wins; empathy for the other person; wonder about what you're missing.
- Use SBIA for feedback: name the situation, the specific behavior, its impact and the action to continue or change.

**5. Don't ship AI unless you understand it yourself**
{: .operating-model-label }

- Can you explain what you're shipping, why the important decisions were made and how you know it works?
- You don't have to read every line of code. You do have to understand the work yourself. Diagnose a process before automating it.

**6. Observe customer emotion**
{: .operating-model-label }

- What did the customer feel? What need is behind that feeling? What's causing it?
- Customers are never wrong about what they feel. Notice your own friction too; you're a customer. Use the feeling to diagnose the problem rather than taking the requested solution at face value.

**7. Increase the talent density**
{: .operating-model-label }

- Does this hire raise the bar? Resist the urge to put butts in seats.
- Increase talent density by coaching the people already here. Be specific about what better looks like and help them get there.
- Use the keeper test: if this person wanted to leave, would we fight to keep them? If not, decide how to coach them, change their role or help them find the right landing place.

**8. Play to win**
{: .operating-model-label }

- Be explicit about what winning means and what it will take. If the current plan won't get us there, change it.
- If we say we care about growth, our priorities, time and staffing should reflect that.

**9. Push the frontier**
{: .operating-model-label }

- How do we want the world to feel? Work backward from that experience to what we need to build.
- Push the frontier on every surface. Reuse what works, and invent where the experience we want requires it.
